# US–China Beijing Summit — May 2026 (Source Summary)

**Summary**: Source summary for the Trump–Xi Jinping bilateral summit in Beijing, May 14–15, 2026 — first US presidential visit to Beijing in nearly a decade. Result: stabilisation, not breakthrough. Near-term geopolitical risk reduced; structural tensions fully intact.

**Last updated**: 2026-05-21

## What Happened

Trump and Xi met in Beijing for two days. The meeting was historic in optics but thin in substance. Key outcome: the existing tariff truce (from November 2025) was preserved and extended to **November 10, 2026** — the most important concrete deliverable.

## Agreed

- China to buy 200 Boeing jets (vs. 500 expected; Boeing fell 4%)
- White House claimed China agreed to "address" US rare earth shortages — no formal deal confirmed
- Agricultural purchase commitments (vague, unconfirmed by China)
- ~750,000 NVIDIA H200 chips licensed to ~10 Chinese tech firms
- Boards of Trade and Investment established
- Strategic stability framework for next 3 years

## Not Agreed

- Tariffs: Trump said they "never came up meaningfully"
- Rare earths: exports still ~50% below pre-control levels; no confirmed agreement
- Taiwan: Xi warned of "clashes and even conflicts" if mishandled; US unchanged
- Iran: no formal cooperation agreement
- Tech decoupling: no reversal framework

## Gold Market Impact

Gold fell from ~$4,774 (pre-summit high) to ~$4,553–$4,652 post-summit as the acute escalation risk premium was reduced. See [[gold-geopolitical-risk-premium]].

Key counterpoint: PBoC buying gold for **17 consecutive months** — summit changes nothing about structural demand.
