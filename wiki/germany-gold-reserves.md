# Germany — Bundesbank Gold Reserves

**Summary**: Germany holds **3,352 tonnes** of gold — the world's second-largest reserve after the USA. The Bundesbank completed a landmark repatriation from Paris and New York in 2017, but ~50% of its gold remains stored abroad (New York + London). In 2026, renewed political calls to bring the New York gold home are growing louder — partly inspired by France's successful $15B repatriation in early 2026.

Germany's gold is split across **three locations**. Frankfurt became the largest single storage site after the 2012–2017 repatriation programme was completed ahead of schedule.

---

## The 2012–2017 Repatriation Programme

Germany's first major repatriation was triggered by a 2012 audit controversy — the German Federal Court of Auditors criticised the Bundesbank for not physically inspecting its gold held abroad. Public pressure led to a formal repatriation plan:

- **Target**: Move 300t from FRBNY (New York) and all 374t from Banque de France (Paris) to Frankfurt by 2020
- **Outcome**: Completed **3 years ahead of schedule** in 2017 — all Paris gold returned, 300t from New York moved
- **Result**: Frankfurt became the Bundesbank's largest single storage site for the first time

The Bundesbank explicitly said at the time it remained comfortable with New York and London as long-term storage locations.

## 2026: Renewed Calls to Leave New York

In early 2026, the debate has re-opened — more urgently than before:

**January 2026**: Former senior Bundesbank official **Emanuel Mönch** stated publicly that storing ~1,236t in the United States was now "dangerous" given the geopolitical unpredictability of the second Trump administration. He called on the Bundesbank to consider further repatriation.

**March 2026**: The **AfD (Alternative for Germany)** tabled a Bundestag motion calling for ALL German gold to be repatriated from both New York and London.

**The France catalyst**: France's April 2026 announcement that it had pulled all 129t of its FRBNY gold — and made **~$15 billion** doing it (see [[france-gold-reserves]]) — gave the German debate new urgency. Medium (April 2026): "France Made $15 Billion Pulling Its Gold From the US Federal Reserve. Germany and Italy Are Being Asked to Do the Same."

**Current government position**: The CDU-led government has stated repatriation is **not currently being considered**. The Bundesbank's official stance: New York remains a secure and reliable storage location.

**June 11, 2026 — Escalation of political discourse**: FXStreet published analysis "**Germans demand bring our gold home**" — the most prominent mainstream financial media headline on this topic in 2026. The article notes repatriation demand has crossed partisan lines: AfD, Green Party finance spokesperson Katharina Beck, and broader public opinion all now favouring repatriation. The Yahoo Finance headline described Germany as "urged to repatriate £122bn in gold from Trump's 'risky' America." Despite growing pressure, the Merz coalition government remains opposed. No formal Bundestag vote scheduled as of June 16, 2026.

## Why Germany Has So Much Gold Abroad

Germany's gold ended up in New York and London for historical reasons:
1. **Cold War**: West Germany stored gold in Allied-controlled vaults as a security measure — if the Soviet Union invaded, Germany could still access reserves held in the West
2. **Bretton Woods**: Active gold trading with the Federal Reserve in the post-war era meant holding gold close to the market
3. **Legacy**: After Bretton Woods ended, the gold simply stayed where it was; repatriation was expensive and seen as unnecessary

The geopolitical logic that justified foreign storage in 1950–1970 has now reversed: the risk is no longer Soviet invasion, but **US policy unpredictability and sanctions weaponisation** (see [[de-dollarisation]]).

## Significance for Gold Price Prediction

1. **Static holder**: Germany is not buying or selling — neutral for near-term prices
2. **Repatriation risk**: If Germany follows France and repatriates the ~1,236t from New York, it would:
   - Remove ~1,236t from the **FRBNY custodial pool** (tightening New York liquidity)
   - Not change global gold supply — just its location
   - But signal a broader shift in transatlantic trust, which is **bullish for gold sentiment**
3. **The 2026 political pressure is real**: AfD motion + former Bundesbank officials speaking out is a more serious signal than previous rounds of debate
