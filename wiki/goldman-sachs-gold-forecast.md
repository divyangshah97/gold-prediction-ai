## ⚠️ June 22, 2026 — Goldman Sachs CUTS Year-End Target: $5,400 → $4,900

**Goldman Sachs reduced its 2026 year-end gold price target from $5,400 to $4,900/oz** — a $500 cut — the first downward revision since Goldman initiated its bull call. This is the single biggest institutional forecast revision of June 2026.

**Reasons cited:**
1. **Weaker ETF inflows** globally post-FOMC — gold ETF demand faltering
2. **Fed cuts pushed to 2027** — FOMC hawkish dot plot (June 17) removed all 2026 easing; October 2026 hike now in active pricing
3. **Warsh hawkish tone** — PCE revised sharply higher to 3.6% (from 2.7%); commitment to price stability
4. **Downside scenario**: If Fed hikes, Goldman sees gold at $4,400/oz

Goldman is now the **most bearish major bank** on year-end target: JPM $6,000, Deutsche Bank $6,000, UBS $5,500 all above GS $4,900. However, **Morgan Stanley's base case scenario is $4,400** (H2 upside capped at $5,200) — making MS the most bearish on a base-case basis. Goldman $4,900 sits below prior institutional consensus range but above MS base case.

**Market context at time of cut**: Gold $4,197.75 (+1.11% intraday June 22). $4,197.75 is 14.3% below the new $4,900 target — still implies meaningful upside but sharply reduced vs the prior $5,400 benchmark.

## Key May 18, 2026 Report Findings

Goldman revised its central bank demand model significantly on May 18, 2026 (see [[hidden-sovereign-buying]]):

- Identified that UK trade data has been failing to capture London vault outflows since August 2025
- This caused a **72% undercount** in their prior central bank demand estimate
- March 2026 nowcast revised to ~50t/month; April 2026 nowcast now ~80t/month
- Full-year 2026 projection raised to ~720t (~60t/month average)
- Maintained $5,400/oz target despite near-term headwinds (investors may sell gold to raise cash in market stress events)

## What Goldman's Model Rests On

Goldman's bullish case for gold is built on three pillars:

1. **[[Central-bank-gold-demand]]**: The primary driver. Structural, price-inelastic EM central bank buying at 50–60t/month
2. **[[De-dollarisation]]**: Ongoing reserve diversification away from USD, with a long runway
3. **Geopolitical risk premium**: Elevated uncertainty sustaining investment demand alongside official sector demand

## Near-Term Risk Goldman Flagged

Goldman warned that gold could face **near-term downward pressure** if investors are forced to sell liquid assets (including gold) to raise cash during periods of broader market stress. This is a tactical risk, not a structural one — it does not change the underlying demand thesis.
