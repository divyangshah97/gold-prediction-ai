# US–China Trade War

**Summary**: The ongoing US–China trade conflict — centred on tariffs, technology, and critical minerals — is a key driver of global geopolitical uncertainty and a structural support for gold as a safe-haven asset.

**Sources**: `raw/us-china-beijing-summit-2026-05-15.md`, `raw/daily-update-2026-05-21.md`, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/03/us-tariffs-60-economies-dection-301-forced-labor-trade-practices-.html, https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/06/03/trump-administration-announces-new-tariffs-over-use-forced-labor/

**Last updated**: 2026-06-04

---

## Current State (as of June 2026)

The US and China are in a **managed truce**, not a resolution:

- The November 2025 trade truce suspended escalating tariffs and China's October 2025 export-control package
- The truce runs until **November 10, 2026** — after which either side can reactivate measures
- The May 2026 Beijing summit preserved and nominally extended this truce but added no structural resolution

### June 2026: New US-China Board of Trade + Forced Labour Tariffs

**US-China Board of Trade (June 2026)**: Following the May 14–15 Beijing summit, the US government began seeking public comments on the scope of a new US-China Board of Trade — a bilateral mechanism agreed at the summit that could lead to reduced tariff rates. This represents incremental cooperation within the broader truce framework. (source: CNBC June 3, 2026)

**New Forced Labour Tariffs (June 3, 2026)**: The USTR proposed additional tariffs of 10–12.5% on imports from 60 economies over failure to ban goods made with forced labor. China faces a **12.5% additional tariff** proposal. Key notes:
- **Gold and precious metals explicitly exempted** from the tariff scope
- Public comment period runs to July 7, 2026 (tariffs not yet in effect)
- China denied the forced-labour allegations and called for dialogue
- India, Japan, South Korea, Brazil also face 12.5% proposals; EU/UK/Canada face 10%
- This is a separate track from the May truce — it escalates trade friction on the industrial goods side even while the truce remains in place for the original tariff architecture

**Implication for gold**: The new forced-labour tariff proposal injects trade policy uncertainty that historically supports gold as a safe-haven. However, the immediate impact is muted since gold is exempted and the tariffs are not yet in force.

## Key Flashpoints

### Tariffs
- Trump stated tariffs "never came up meaningfully" at the Beijing summit
- No structural tariff agreement was reached
- The tariff architecture from the 2025 trade war remains in place, only suspended

### Rare Earths & Critical Minerals
China's most powerful leverage: it controls ~90% of global rare earth refining. Even during the truce, exports of key minerals remain ~50% below pre-control levels. See [[rare-earths-geopolitics]].

### Technology / Semiconductors
China is being cut off from advanced US chips (with partial exceptions). The NVIDIA H200 licensing of ~750,000 chips announced at the summit is a limited carve-out, not a policy reversal. Xi told Trump China intends to develop its own chips regardless.

### Taiwan
The deepest structural fault line. Xi explicitly warned that mishandling Taiwan would put the relationship "in great jeopardy" and risk "clashes and even conflicts." The US maintained strategic ambiguity without concession. No resolution path exists.

## May 20 Update: China Defends Controls, Tariff Cuts Discussed

**China's Ministry of Commerce (May 20):** Formally stated that rare earth export controls are implemented "in accordance with laws and regulations" — the first public framing of controls as lawful permanent policy, not a temporary measure. Offered vague cooperation on "reasonable" US concerns with no timeline or binding commitment. See [[rare-earths-geopolitics]].

**Tariff cut talks (May 20):** Both sides are weighing matching **$30 billion tariff cuts**, per Euronews reporting. Talks remain vague — no confirmed deal, timeline, or announcement.

## The November 10, 2026 Deadline

This is the single most important near-term geopolitical date for gold markets:

- If the truce is extended: continued de-escalation, mild near-term headwind for safe-haven gold
- If the truce lapses: rare earth export controls reactivate, tariffs resume, acute risk premium returns — **strongly bullish for gold**
- US Trade Representative indicated "willingness on both sides" to extend, but this is not guaranteed

## What the Trade War Means for Gold

The US–China trade war contributes to gold's structural bid through three channels:

1. **Safe-haven demand**: Escalation episodes drive investor flows into gold (see [[gold-geopolitical-risk-premium]])
2. **[[De-dollarisation]] acceleration**: Trade conflict pushes China (and EM allies) to reduce dollar dependence faster, increasing [[central-bank-gold-demand]]
3. **Supply chain disruption risk**: Rare earth controls affect global manufacturing; economic uncertainty supports gold

Even during periods of truce/de-escalation, the structural drivers of gold accumulation (de-dollarisation, reserve diversification) continue uninterrupted. The PBoC bought gold for 17 consecutive months through May 2026 — diplomatic temperature has no correlation with its accumulation pace.
