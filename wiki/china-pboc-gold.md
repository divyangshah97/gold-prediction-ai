# China — People's Bank of China Gold Reserves

**Summary**: The PBoC is one of the world's largest gold holders and a consistent buyer, but its reported purchases likely understate actual accumulation due to opacity and the phenomenon of [[hidden-sovereign-buying]].

China holds the world's 6th largest gold reserves by official count, but its gold-to-reserve ratio (~9%) remains far below Western central banks (US: ~70%, Germany: ~70%, Italy: ~68%). This gap suggests significant room to accumulate further.

### April 2026 Update

On May 8, 2026, the PBoC reported an **8.1t gold addition in April 2026** — the highest single-month purchase since December 2024, extending China's consecutive buying streak to **18 months**. Total official holdings reached **2,322 tonnes**, representing 9% of China's total FX reserves ($3.8 trillion). The WGC China gold market update (May 2026) characterised this as a "notable rise in gold reserves." (source: ZeroHedge/PBoC May 8 2026, WGC May 2026)

## Why China's Reported Data Is Likely an Undercount

China's PBoC is one of the most opaque central banks in the world on gold reporting:

1. **Infrequent disclosure**: China has a history of announcing large reserve increases infrequently (e.g., it added 600t in a single 2015 announcement after years of silence).
2. **[[Hidden-sovereign-buying]]**: Goldman Sachs's May 2026 revised model, which found ~21t/month of unaccounted global buying, is widely believed to include significant Chinese accumulation. China has motivation to avoid telegraphing purchases that would move prices.
3. **State-owned entity routing**: Chinese purchases may flow through state banks and sovereign funds before appearing on PBoC balance sheets, further obscuring real-time demand.

## Strategic Motivation

China's gold buying is driven by:
- **[[De-dollarisation]]**: Reducing USD reserve dependence in the context of trade tensions and sanctions risk
- **Yuan internationalisation**: Gold backing (or at least the perception of it) supports credibility for the yuan as a reserve currency
- **Geopolitical hedging**: Insurance against potential financial sanctions similar to those applied to Russia in 2022

## Significance for Gold Price Prediction

PBoC buying is a **wildcard upside factor**. Official data shows modest incremental buying (7t in Q1 2026), but true demand may be multiples higher. Any future large disclosure announcement could be a strong bullish catalyst. The combination of long-term accumulation motivation and opacity makes China one of the hardest demand signals to model but also one of the most important.

## Diplomatic Insulation

The May 2026 Trump–Xi Beijing summit ([[us-china-beijing-summit-may-2026]]) had no bearing on PBoC gold buying. Despite two days of diplomatic engagement, the PBoC's 17-month consecutive buying streak continued. This confirms that China's gold accumulation is a **policy-level strategic decision**, not a tactical market response. The [[us-china-trade-war]] context — de-dollarisation, sanctions risk, yuan internationalisation — drives the buying regardless of short-term diplomatic temperature.
