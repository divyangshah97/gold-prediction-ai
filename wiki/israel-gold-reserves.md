# Israel — Bank of Israel Gold Reserves

**Summary**: Israel holds **zero gold reserves** — a deliberate policy since the 1980s when the Bank of Israel sold all its gold under then-Governor Stanley Fischer. With total FX reserves of ~$214 billion (2024), Israel is one of the world's largest reserve holders per capita but keeps none in gold, preferring equities and bonds. This policy is now under active political debate as gold hits record highs.

Israel is unusual in combining very large total reserves with a zero-gold allocation — most large reserve holders maintain at least some gold.

---

## How Israel Got to Zero

The Bank of Israel sold its gold reserves in the **1980s**, when **Stanley Fischer** served as Bank of Israel Governor (he later became vice-chair of the US Federal Reserve). Fischer's rationale was pragmatic:
- Gold was seen as expensive to store and yielding no return
- FX reserves in USD, EUR, and equities were seen as more liquid and productive
- The gold standard era was over; gold's monetary role had diminished

The Bank of Israel has maintained the zero-gold position ever since — through multiple wars, intifadas, diplomatic crises, and now the 2026 Iran conflict.

---

## Current Investment Strategy

Israel's $214+ billion in reserves (one of the world's largest per capita) is invested primarily in:
- **US, European, and other government bonds**
- **Global equities** — the Bank of Israel has a large equity allocation, unusual for a central bank
- **Foreign currencies** (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CNY)

Bank of Israel officials have argued that their **equity-heavy strategy has returned roughly 3x more than gold** over the long run, justifying the zero-gold approach.

---

## The 2025–2026 Political Debate

As gold hit all-time highs (~$5,200/oz in March 2026), Israel's zero-gold policy became a hot political topic:

- The **Knesset Finance Committee** held a rare hearing on whether the Bank of Israel should have bought gold when prices surged
- Lawmakers argued the bank "missed out" on enormous gains
- Bank of Israel officials pushed back, defending the equities strategy as superior long-term
- No formal proposal to change the policy has been tabled

The debate is largely retrospective — even if Israel decided to buy gold today, at $4,500+/oz, the entry point would be far above historical prices, making the political calculus difficult.

---

## The Geopolitical Paradox

Israel's zero-gold stance is striking given its geopolitical position:
- Israel faces existential threats and has been in a near-constant state of regional conflict
- Gold is widely held as a **non-confiscatable safe-haven asset** — exactly the kind of insurance small, geopolitically exposed nations use
- The [[iran-conflict-2026]] has directly affected Israel's security environment in 2026
- Most comparable-sized nations under geopolitical stress (e.g., [[israel]] peer set) hold significant gold

Yet the Bank of Israel's position is that **financial returns matter more than geopolitical insurance** — a view that may be reconsidered if Israel ever faces financial sanctions or USD payment system exclusion.

---

## Significance for Gold Price Prediction

Israel's position is **neutral for gold prices** — it is neither buying nor selling. However:
- If the Bank of Israel ever reversed policy and began buying gold (even modestly, 20–30t), it would be a notable signal of a broader geopolitical re-evaluation
- The Knesset debate suggests domestic political pressure is building — worth monitoring
- Israel's $214B reserve base means even a 5% gold allocation would require **~240 tonnes** of purchases — significant demand
