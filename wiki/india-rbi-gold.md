# India — RBI Gold Reserves

**Summary**: India's RBI holds 880.52 tonnes of gold (March 2026) but has nearly stopped buying new gold after a peak 72.6t acquisition in 2024. The dominant 2026 story is large-scale repatriation of gold from London back to domestic vaults.

**Sources**: `raw/india-russia-gold-reserves-2026-05-21.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-05-21

## Current Position (March 2026)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total gold reserves | 880.52 tonnes |
| Domestic (held in India) | 680.05 tonnes |
| Overseas (BoE, BIS) | 197.67 tonnes |
| Gold as % of FX reserves | ~13.9% (Sep 2025) |
| FX reserves total value | >$691 billion |

## Historical Buying Pace

| Period | Net Purchases |
|---|---|
| 2024 | 72.6 tonnes — peak year |
| 2025 | 4 tonnes — lowest in 8 years |
| Q1 2026 | ~0.34 tonnes (essentially flat) |

India front-loaded its accumulation. The pace has structurally slowed — not because of reduced conviction, but because the RBI achieved its strategic buying goals in 2024 and is now managing its existing position.

## The Real 2026 Story: Repatriation

India is not buying much new gold in 2026, but it is aggressively **moving its existing gold home**:

- ~168 tonnes repatriated in the 12 months to March 2026 alone
- Overseas holdings fell from 437t (March 2023) → 197t (March 2026)
- Gold is being returned from Bank of England and BIS vaults

**Why repatriating?** The 2022 freezing of Russia's foreign assets was a turning point. India is reducing its custodial exposure to Western institutions — keeping gold physically in India means it cannot be seized or frozen regardless of geopolitical developments. This is [[de-dollarisation]] logic applied to custody, not just ownership.

## Why the Gold Price Has Still Gone Up Despite Low Buying

India's gold reserves value rose by ~$31 billion in FY26, not from new purchases, but from gold price appreciation. Gold's share of total FX reserves rose from ~9% (Sep 2024) to ~13.9% (Sep 2025) purely due to price. This illustrates that even without new buying, existing holders contribute to price support through reduced selling pressure.

## Significance for Gold Price Prediction

India's stance is **neutral-to-mildly bullish** for gold:
- Not a new supply of demand (barely buying)
- Not a source of selling pressure
- Repatriation removes gold from London's liquidity pool, tightening physical supply in the West
- If gold prices correct significantly, the RBI is a credible large buyer (demonstrated by 2024)

The repatriation trend also signals that large sovereign holders are reducing trust in Western custodians — a structural theme shared with [[china-pboc-gold]] and [[poland-gold-reserves]].
