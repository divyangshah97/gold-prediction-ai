# Central Bank Gold Demand

**Summary**: Central banks are the single largest source of structural gold demand, collectively buying hundreds of tonnes per year as they diversify reserves away from the US dollar.

**Sources**: `raw/central-bank-gold-purchases-2026-05-19.md`, `raw/daily-update-2026-05-21.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-05-21

---

## Scale of Demand

Central bank net purchases have become a dominant force in the gold market since 2022. In Q1 2026, global central banks bought a net **244 tonnes** — up 17% from Q4 2025 and 3% from Q1 2025.

Full-year 2026 forecasts:
- **World Gold Council**: ~850 tonnes
- **Goldman Sachs**: ~720 tonnes annualised (60t/month), with possible acceleration

Goldman Sachs revised their nowcast upward to **~60 t/month** (12-month moving average, as of May 2026), up from 50t/month (May 18 revision) and 29t/month under the prior methodology. The original revision was caused by [[hidden-sovereign-buying]] — unreported purchases masked by gaps in UK trade data.

## Who Is Selling

Notable net sellers in Q1 2026:
- **Turkey**: ~70t reduction (mostly via gold swaps, not outright sales; stabilised in April). See [[turkey-gold-reserves]].
- **Azerbaijan (SOFAZ)**: 22t
- **Russia**: 22t (~21.8t YTD through April per Bank of Russia; driven by war-related fiscal deficit — forced liquidation, not a bearish price view). See [[russia-gold-reserves]].
- **Kyrgyz Republic**: 1t

Turkey's selling is a financing mechanism; Russia's is sanctions-driven fiscal liquidation. Neither reflects a bearish view on gold. Russia's ~22t/quarter is fully absorbed by Poland's 31t in Q1 alone.

## Why It Matters for Gold Prices

Central bank demand is **price-inelastic** — banks buy for strategic reasons (diversification, geopolitical hedging, de-dollarisation) rather than in response to price. This makes it a reliable structural floor under gold prices. [[goldman-sachs-gold-forecast]] cites official demand as the primary support for its $5,400/oz end-2026 target.

## Key Drivers

- Reserve diversification away from USD
- Geopolitical risk (Ukraine, Taiwan, sanctions risk)
- [[de-dollarisation]] — reducing dependence on dollar-denominated assets
- Inflation protection
- Portfolio performance during financial stress
