# De-dollarisation

**Summary**: The global trend of central banks and governments reducing reliance on the US dollar in reserves and trade, a key structural driver of gold demand.

**Sources**: `raw/central-bank-gold-purchases-2026-05-19.md`, `raw/us-china-beijing-summit-2026-05-15.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-05-21

---

## What It Is

De-dollarisation refers to the deliberate reduction of USD exposure in foreign exchange reserves, trade invoicing, and financial infrastructure. Gold is the primary alternative reserve asset because it carries no counterparty risk, cannot be frozen by sanctions, and is universally accepted.

## Why It's Accelerating

The 2022 freezing of Russian sovereign assets held in Western financial institutions was a turning point. It demonstrated that dollar-denominated assets are subject to geopolitical seizure risk. This prompted central banks — particularly in emerging markets — to accelerate gold accumulation as a sanction-proof store of value.

Additional drivers:
- US fiscal deficit concerns undermining long-run dollar credibility
- Multipolar geopolitical environment reducing trust in US-centric financial infrastructure
- EM countries seeking monetary sovereignty

## How It Shows Up in the Data

[[central-bank-gold-demand]] data reflects this directly:
- EM central banks (Poland, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, China, Malaysia, Cambodia, Indonesia, UAE) dominated Q1 2026 purchases
- **244 tonnes** net purchased in Q1 2026
- Goldman Sachs forecasts **~60t/month** average for 2026

Poland's buying (31t in Q1 alone, targeting 700t total) explicitly references security and diversification from EUR/USD exposure. See [[poland-gold-reserves]].

China's PBoC made its largest single-month gold purchase in 17 months sometime in Q1 2026, suggesting accelerating de-dollarisation at the world's second-largest economy. See [[china-pboc-gold]].

## Significance for Gold Price Prediction

De-dollarisation creates **structural, price-inelastic demand** for gold. Unlike investor demand (which responds to rates, inflation expectations, and sentiment), central bank buying driven by de-dollarisation is driven by policy goals with multi-year horizons. This means:

- Gold price dips are likely bought by central banks, providing a floor
- The trend is unlikely to reverse quickly even if geopolitical tensions ease
- [[goldman-sachs-gold-forecast]] treats this as the primary pillar of its $5,400/oz target

## The Trade War Accelerant

The [[us-china-trade-war]] is actively accelerating de-dollarisation. The May 2026 Beijing summit produced no structural resolution — tariffs remain suspended (not removed), rare earth leverage is intact, and Taiwan is unresolved. China's strategic calculus for holding gold over dollars strengthens, not weakens, as long as the US can use financial infrastructure as a weapon. The summit confirmed that the de-dollarisation thesis has a very long runway.
