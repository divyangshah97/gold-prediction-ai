# USA — Federal Reserve Gold Reserves

**Summary**: The United States holds **8,133 tonnes** of gold — the world's largest official gold reserve, confirmed as of April 30, 2026 (US Treasury/FRED). The US has not bought or sold gold since 1971 and values its holdings at the outdated statutory price of $42.22/oz; at market price (~$4,500/oz), the true value is ~$1.17 trillion.

**Sources**: Web research — [US Treasury FRED April 2026](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/release/tables?eid=155805&rid=426), [US Treasury Fiscal Data](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/status-report-government-gold-reserve/), [Metalorix](https://metalorix.com/en/learn/price-factors/us-gold-reserves-and-fort-knox-8133-tonnes-explained), [Wikipedia Fort Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bullion_Depository)

## Why the US Holds So Much Gold

The US accumulated its position during the **Bretton Woods era (1944–1971)**, when the dollar was pegged to gold at $35/oz and every other currency pegged to the dollar. The US was the anchor of the entire global monetary system — and gold was the anchor of the dollar.

After Nixon ended dollar-gold convertibility in August 1971 ("Nixon Shock"), the US stopped transacting in gold but retained its entire position. It is now a **passive holder** — neither buying nor selling.

---

## The $42.22/oz Fiction

The US government still values its gold at the **statutory price of $42.22/oz**, set by Congress in 1973. This is a purely accounting fiction:
- At market price (~$4,500/oz), true value is **~$1.17 trillion** — 106x the book value
- This creates a massive **hidden asset** on the US government's balance sheet
- Periodic proposals to "revalue" gold to market price have circulated — doing so would generate a paper windfall on paper that could technically reduce the deficit without selling any gold
- No such revaluation has been enacted

The **Federal Reserve Bank of New York** also holds gold **on behalf of foreign central banks and international organisations** — this is separate from US-owned gold and represents custody, not US reserves.

## Audit Controversy

The last **full physical audit of US gold** was conducted in **1953** under Eisenhower. Since then:
- Regular congressional calls for a new audit have been deflected or partially completed
- The GAO has conducted partial audits of specific vaults
- Some analysts (and Mises Institute commentators) note that Fort Knox gold may be "coin melt" quality — not the 999.9 fine bars required for international settlement
- There is no confirmed evidence of fraud, but the opacity is a recurring concern among gold advocates

## Geopolitical Dimension

The US gold reserve is the **backing of last resort for dollar credibility**. Several interconnected dynamics:

1. **Sanctions as gold accelerant**: The 2022 freezing of Russia's $300B in dollar reserves accelerated gold buying globally. The US weaponisation of the dollar payment system is the single biggest driver of EM [[de-dollarisation]] and central bank gold accumulation worldwide
2. **Dollar hegemony dependency**: As long as the dollar remains the global reserve currency, the US gold pile is largely symbolic. If that status erodes, 8,133t becomes a critical anchor
3. **FRBNY custodial role**: The US holds foreign nations' gold in New York — this is a geopolitical lever. Countries that don't trust US intentions (Russia, China, Iran) have been moving their gold OUT of US custody

---

## Significance for Gold Price Prediction

The US gold position is **structurally inert** for price purposes:
- Not buying → no new demand signal
- Not selling → no supply pressure
- **Key sensitivity**: US Fed policy (see [[fed-macro-factors]]) and **USD strength** (see [[gold-geopolitical-risk-premium]]) are the real US-driven gold price factors

If the US ever revalued its gold to market price (~$1.17T), or used it as collateral in a new monetary arrangement, the market impact would be enormous. Not a near-term scenario but worth understanding.
