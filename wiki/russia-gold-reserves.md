# Russia — Gold Reserves

**Summary**: Russia holds ~2,305 tonnes of gold and has been a net seller since November 2025, driven entirely by wartime fiscal pressure. All current selling happens on Russia's domestic market — no Russian gold has been exported outside the Eurasian Economic Union since April 2023. This page covers accumulation history, the two-pot reserve structure, the selling mechanism, G7 sanctions enforcement, domestic gold production, and the depletion trajectory.

## The Two-Pot Structure: NWF vs Bank of Russia

Russia's gold exists across two separate but overlapping institutional structures. Understanding this is essential to interpreting news about "Russia selling gold."

### Bank of Russia (BoR) — International Reserves
- The headline figure: ~2,305t as of mid-2026
- The BoR holds gold as part of Russia's total international reserves (~$713B total as of Oct 2025)
- The BoR is the **operational manager** of the gold (physically stores, executes sales)
- Since Feb 2022: $300B in BoR's dollar/euro/sterling reserves were frozen by G7 — these are gone. Gold was not frozen because it sits in Russia, not Western custodians.

### National Wealth Fund (NWF) — Finance Ministry's Sovereign Wealth Fund
- A **separate entity** managed by Russia's Ministry of Finance; BoR acts as custodian
- Purpose: a fiscal buffer to fund budget deficits, recapitalize state banks, subsidize industries
- NWF gold is a **subset** of the total BoR reserve figure above

| Date | NWF Gold | NWF Total Value |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 2022 | ~405t (peak) | $113.5B |
| Oct 2024 | 292.5t | — |
| Feb 1, 2026 | **155.1t** | ~$51.6B (total NWF) |

The NWF has lost ~137t of gold in just 16 months (Oct 2024 → Feb 2026) — a dramatic depletion driven by wartime fiscal needs. The NWF's total value halved from $113.5B (2022) to $51.6B (2025).

**The selling mechanism:** When the Finance Ministry needs rubles to fund the deficit, it instructs the BoR to sell NWF gold on the domestic Russian market. The BoR executes the trade; rubles flow back to the Finance Ministry's budget. This is what WGC reports as Bank of Russia "gold sales."

## Accumulation History: 2006–2022

Russia's gold position is not an accident — it was a deliberate 16-year strategic build-up orchestrated by Putin.

### Timeline

| Period | Action | Cumulative Total |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-2006 | Minimal holdings, low priority | ~400t |
| 2006–2013 | Steady accumulation begins | ~1,000t |
| **2014 — Crimea sanctions** | Putin dramatically accelerates buying | ~1,200t |
| 2014–2020 | BoR buys ~150–250t/year; suspends USD reserves accumulation | ~2,300t |
| March 2022 | BoR **suspends buying** from miners (reserves sufficient; war starts) | ~2,330t peak |
| Nov 2025 | BoR begins selling for first time | ~2,326t |

### Three Strategic Goals

The Bank of Russia's buying programme addressed three explicit objectives:

1. **De-dollarise reserves** — reduce exposure to the "weaponisable" US dollar. Russia steadily sold US Treasuries and replaced them with gold. Gold cannot be frozen, seized via SWIFT, or blocked by the US Treasury.
2. **Domestic liquidity without SWIFT** — gold held inside Russia can be exchanged for rubles without going through international banking infrastructure. This proved prescient in Feb 2022.
3. **Support domestic gold miners** — by providing guaranteed domestic demand, the BoR gave Polyus, Polymetal, and others a stable buyer. This kept the industry viable and inside Russia.

### Putin's Prescient Call

When the G7 froze Russia's ~$300B in dollar/euro/sterling reserves in Feb–Mar 2022, Russia's gold was untouched — because it sits in BoR vaults in Moscow, not in New York, London, or Brussels. The 2006–2022 accumulation strategy was specifically designed to create a reserve that couldn't be frozen. It worked exactly as intended: Russia entered the war with $140B in gold that remained fully accessible even as its other reserves were locked.

### The 5,000 RUB/Gram Peg (March 2022)

In March 2022, immediately after G7 sanctions hit, the BoR set a fixed buying price of **5,000 rubles per gram** (~$52/gram vs market ~$62/gram = **~19% below spot**). This was not a true gold standard (citizens could not redeem rubles for gold). It was a price-support mechanism for domestic miners who had suddenly lost access to G7 export markets. The BoR stopped buying from miners by April 2022 — reserves were already sufficient — but the fixed price announcement helped stabilise the market and signalled BoR's commitment to the domestic gold sector.

## The Selling Mechanism (November 2025 onward)

For the first time since 2002, the Bank of Russia began selling physical gold from its reserves in **November 2025**.

### How Domestic Sales Work

1. Russia's federal budget deficit reached ~$61.3B by end of Q1 2026, driven by wartime spending
2. With dollar/euro reserves frozen, the Finance Ministry's only liquid tools are: gold, Chinese yuan, ruble-denominated instruments
3. Finance Ministry instructs BoR to sell NWF gold on the **domestic Russian market**
4. BoR sells physical gold to Russian commercial banks, state companies, and select investment entities
5. Buyers pay in rubles → rubles flow to Finance Ministry → fund the budget deficit
6. **Gold stays inside Russia** — it transfers from BoR vaults to commercial bank vaults in Russia

This is the critical insight: **Russia's current gold sales do not enter international markets.** The metal changes hands within Russia's domestic financial system. There is no LBMA, COMEX, or bullion bank intermediary involved.

### Sales Pace

| Period | Volume | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 16 – Feb 5, 2026 | Heavy sales | 12.8B RUB/day (fastest pace yet) |
| Jan–Apr 2026 (WGC) | ~22t total | ~5.5t/month |
| May 2026 | ~6t | ~6t/month |
| Projected 2026 full year | 80–90t (if current pace holds) | ~7t/month |

## G7 Gold Ban and Export Routes (2022–2023)

### The Ban

On **June 26, 2022**, the G7 (US, UK, EU, Canada, Japan) announced a ban on importing Russian-origin gold. This was the first direct sanction targeting Russia's gold exports.

**Loopholes in the original ban:**
- Gold already physically outside Russia before the ban date was exempt
- Gold "substantially transformed" in a third country (alloyed, re-refined) lost its Russian-origin designation and could re-enter G7 markets

### Circumvention Routes (2022–Early 2023)

Russia moved quickly to exploit the loopholes and redirect gold flows:

| Route | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **UAE direct** | Russian banks (Lanta Bank, Vitabank) received 21 shipments of cash (USD, EUR, AED, CNY) worth $82M from UAE and Turkey in exchange for Russian gold |
| **Armenia transit** | Gold shipped Russia → Armenia (EAEU member, no re-export controls at the time) → UAE → onward |
| **Turkey** | Direct cash-for-gold trade; Turkey remained outside the G7 ban |
| **China/Hong Kong** | Smaller flows; China willing buyer but cautious about secondary sanctions risk |

### Enforcement Tightens

By **April 2023, all gold export flows from Russia outside the EAEU had ceased.** The combination of secondary sanctions risk (US/EU threatening any entity buying Russian gold), UK National Crime Agency red alerts (late 2023), and UAE compliance pressure shut down the major transit routes.

Since April 2023: **no Russian gold has been exported outside the Eurasian Economic Union.** Russia is now essentially selling gold domestically only.

## Russia's Domestic Gold Production

Russia is the **world's 2nd largest gold producer** (behind China), and this supply base is intimately connected to its reserve strategy.

### Production Figures

| Year | Volume | Global Rank |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | ~330t | 2nd (behind China 380t) |
| 2025 | 345t (independent est.) / 480–485t (Russian official claim) | 2nd–1st (disputed) |
| 2026 | Projected 480–500t (official); contested by analysts | — |

The large discrepancy in 2025/2026 figures is unresolved. Independent consultancy Metals Focus puts 2025 output at 345t; Russian government officials claim 480–485t. No major new mine entered production that would explain a 40%+ jump.

### Key Producers

- **Polyus** — Russia's largest, primarily in Krasnoyarsk, Irkutsk, Magadan, Yakutia. Olimpiada mine alone produced 926,000 oz (28.8t) in 2025.
- **Polymetal** — Gold and silver; Far East and Khabarovsk Krai. Restructured during sanctions, moved domicile to Kazakhstan.
- **Sukhoi Log (undeveloped)** — One of the world's largest untapped gold deposits, being developed by Polyus. Expected to more than double Polyus's output when operational — but not until end of this decade.

### How Production Flows into Reserves

Historically (2006–2022): BoR bought ~150–250t/year directly from domestic miners at market prices. This was the primary mechanism for building reserves.

Since March 2022: BoR **suspended buying from miners**. Miners now sell through:
1. Russian commercial banks (primary domestic route)
2. Non-G7 export markets (China, UAE, India via third-country routes)
3. Domestic jewelry and industrial demand

The suspension of BoR buying meant miners lost their guaranteed buyer — one reason the 5,000 RUB/gram peg mattered so much to the industry in early 2022.

## Why Russia Is Selling

**Primary driver: fiscal survival under sanctions.** Russia cannot access its frozen dollar/euro reserves. Oil revenues are under pressure as Urals crude trades at a discount and G7 price cap enforcement tightens. Gold and yuan are its only fully liquid international assets, and gold is uniquely useful for domestic liquidity.

**Official stated reason**: Portfolio rebalancing — gold's price appreciation (gold +72% since Jan 2022) increased its share of total reserves beyond target levels, requiring "diversification."

**Both are true**: The rebalancing rationale is arithmetically accurate, but the budget deficit is the forcing function. The Finance Ministry is not selling because it thinks gold will fall — it is selling because it has no better option.

## Why This Is Not a Bearish Market Signal for Gold

Three reasons Russia's selling is structurally irrelevant to global gold prices:

1. **Sales are domestic** — Since April 2023, all Russian gold sales occur inside Russia's domestic market. The gold goes from BoR vaults to Russian bank vaults. It does not enter the LBMA, CME, or any international pricing venue. Market impact: **zero.**

2. **Pace is trivial vs. global buyers** — Even at the projected 80–90t/year 2026 pace, Russia's selling is fully offset by Poland alone (~60–70t YTD 2026) and is a rounding error against global CB net buying (~1,200t in 2025 per WGC).

3. **No market signal about gold's price outlook** — Russia is not selling because it expects prices to fall. It is selling because sanctions removed all its other options. If sanctions were lifted tomorrow, Russia would likely resume buying aggressively — as it did after every prior sanctions episode.

Compare to [[turkey-gold-reserves]]: Turkey's Q1 2026 reduction was a transient financing mechanism. Russia's selling is more structural (multi-year budget necessity) but equally orthogonal to gold's fundamental outlook.

## Depletion Trajectory

### NWF Gold (Finance Ministry Pool)

| Date | NWF Gold | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 2022 peak | ~405t | — |
| Oct 2024 | 292.5t | −112.5t over ~3 years |
| Feb 1, 2026 | **155.1t** | −137.4t in 16 months (acceleration) |
| At 8t/month pace (2026 projected) | ~59t by end 2026 | NWF gold largely depleted by 2027 |

The NWF gold pool (~155t as of Feb 2026) is the primary source of current sales. At the current pace, the Finance Ministry's gold buffer could be exhausted within 1–2 years.

### Total BoR Reserves

| Scenario | Annual Selling | Years to 2,000t |
|---|---|---|
| Current WGC pace (~66t/year) | ~66t | ~5 years |
| Projected 2026 pace (~84t/year) | ~84t | ~4 years |
| Ukrainian intelligence estimate (230t/year) | ~230t | ~1.3 years to 2,000t |

The 230t/year figure (from Ukrainian intelligence, likely referring to 2025 NWF depletion including non-gold assets) is almost certainly an overstatement for physical BoR gold. The WGC-verified figures (22t lost Jan–Apr 2026) are the most reliable basis.

At ~84t/year, Russia would reach 2,000t (still a massive position) only by ~2030. The total reserve is not existentially threatened, but **the NWF gold buffer — the Finance Ministry's crisis tool — is depleting rapidly.**

### Key Risk: Acceleration

If oil prices fall further, Urals crude discount widens, or the war continues longer than budgeted, selling could accelerate well beyond current pace. Russia's budget was built on an oil price assumption; if that assumption breaks, gold selling is the lever.
