## Why Russia Is Selling

**Primary driver: fiscal pressure.** Russia's federal budget deficit reached $61.3 billion, driven by wartime spending. Under Western sanctions, Russia cannot access its dollar or euro assets — gold and yuan are its only liquid reserve assets. Selling gold on the open market (or to domestic buyers) is one of the few channels to fund government spending.

**Official stated reason**: Rebalancing — gold's price appreciation has increased its proportion of total reserves beyond target, requiring "diversification."

**Both are true**: The price-driven rebalancing rationale has merit, but the fiscal necessity is the deeper driver.

## Why This Is Not a Bearish Signal for Gold

Russia's selling is **forced liquidation under sanctions**, not a market call:

1. Russia has no choice about which assets to liquidate — gold is one of the few it can access
2. The selling pace (~22t/quarter) is small relative to global central bank buying (~244t in Q1 2026 alone)
3. Russia is not selling because it expects gold to fall — it is selling because it has no alternative
4. If sanctions were lifted tomorrow, Russia would likely resume accumulation

Compare this to [[turkey-gold-reserves]]: Turkey's Q1 2026 reduction was also misread as bearish but was a transient financing mechanism. Russia's selling is more structural (ongoing budget need) but equally not a reflection of bearish price views.

## Net Impact on the Market

Russia's ~22t/quarter selling is fully absorbed by Poland's 31t in Q1 alone. At the global [[central-bank-gold-demand]] level, Russia is a rounding error on the sell side against the structural EM buying wave.
