# Italy — Banca d'Italia Gold Reserves

**Summary**: Italy holds **2,452 tonnes** of gold — the world's 3rd largest reserve, more than France and just behind Germany. A major political ownership dispute erupted in late 2025, with Meloni's Fratelli d'Italia asserting the gold "belongs to the State," triggering ECB pushback. Italy is also under growing pressure to follow France's $15B repatriation playbook and pull its gold from New York.

## The 2025–2026 Ownership Dispute

This is the most significant Italian gold story in decades.

### The Amendment

During Senate scrutiny of Italy's **2026 Budget** (November 2025), Senator **Lucio Malan** of **Fratelli d'Italia** (Prime Minister Meloni's party) introduced an amendment stating:

> *"The gold reserves managed and held by the Bank of Italy belong to the State, in the name of the Italian people."*

This was backed by coalition partner **Lega** (League). The amendment did not propose selling the gold — only clarifying who owns it.

### Why It's Legally Explosive

Under the **EU Eurosystem framework**:
- National central banks (like Banca d'Italia) are **legally independent** of their governments
- Gold and FX reserves are monetary assets that underpin the **credibility of the euro** — they belong to the monetary system, not the state treasury
- Transferring legal ownership of gold to the Italian state would violate:
  - The **prohibition on monetary financing** (governments cannot use CB assets to fund spending)
  - The principle of **central bank independence** (enshrined in EU treaties)
  - ECB governance rules

### ECB's Response

- ECB President **Christine Lagarde** stated: "Bankitalia has full authority over reserves"
- The ECB formally expressed **doubts about the amendment**, urging Rome to reconsider
- ECB statement: there is no "concrete purpose" for transferring legal ownership
- Economist **Lucrezia Reichlin** warned in Project Syndicate that the move "could destabilise the eurozone"

### Current Status (May 2026)

The amendment has **not been enacted** — it was blocked in the face of ECB opposition. However:
- The political pressure from within Meloni's coalition has not gone away
- The French $15B repatriation (see [[france-gold-reserves]]) has intensified Italian calls for at least repatriation from New York, even if the ownership dispute remains unresolved
- If Italy cannot legally "spend" the gold, some politicians argue it should at least be **on Italian soil**

## The France Comparison — Why Italy Is Under Pressure

Following France's April 2026 announcement of a ~$15B profit from selling its 129t of FRBNY gold and buying it back in Europe:

- Italy is explicitly mentioned as the next candidate for this strategy
- Italy holds significant gold at the FRBNY in New York in older, non-standard bars — **the same situation France was in**
- A similar upgrade-repatriation could theoretically generate **comparable profits** for Italy
- However, Italy's political paralysis over ownership makes it harder to execute — who authorises the sale? Banca d'Italia (which legally can) or the government (which politically wants to but legally cannot)?

## Italy's Gold Legacy

Italy accumulated its gold reserves during the postwar economic miracle (1950s–1970s), when Italy ran large current account surpluses and accumulated hard currency and gold. Like France and Germany, Italy never pursued aggressive selling — its gold position has been remarkably stable for decades.

Italy's gold-to-reserves ratio (~65–70%) is among the highest in Europe — a legacy of treating gold as permanent monetary insurance rather than a trading asset.

## Significance for Gold Price Prediction

1. **Static holder**: Italy is not buying or selling — neutral for near-term prices
2. **Ownership dispute risk**: If Italy's ownership law passes (unlikely near-term), and the government tries to use gold to fund spending, it would be:
   - A severe ECB/EU legal crisis
   - A potential trigger for Italian bond spread widening
   - **Bearish for gold** if it resulted in actual sales; **bearish for EUR**
3. **Repatriation upside**: If Italy follows France and executes a profitable repatriation/upgrade, it would add to the home-custody narrative — **bullish sentiment**
4. **Watch signal**: Any Italian parliamentary vote on gold ownership is a risk event worth tracking
