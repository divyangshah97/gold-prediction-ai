# Rare Earths Geopolitics

**Summary**: China's dominance over rare earth supply chains — 90% of global refining, 60%+ of mining — gives it powerful economic leverage over the US and allies, and is a key escalation risk factor for gold markets heading into the November 2026 truce deadline.

**Sources**: `raw/us-china-beijing-summit-2026-05-15.md`, `raw/daily-update-2026-05-21.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-05-21

## China's Dominant Position

| Metric | China's Share |
|---|---|
| Global rare earth refining | ~90% |
| Global mined supply | 60%+ |
| US supply chain resilience timeline | Years away |

Rare earths are critical inputs for:
- Electric vehicles (motors, batteries)
- Defence systems (guidance, radar, communications)
- Semiconductors and advanced electronics
- Renewable energy (wind turbines, solar)

## Specific Minerals Under Export Controls

China began restricting exports of specific minerals in October 2025. As of May 2026, exports of these minerals remain ~50% below pre-control levels **despite the active trade truce**:

- **Dysprosium** — critical for EV motors and military hardware
- **Terbium** — used in solid-state devices and green energy tech
- **Yttrium** — used in lasers, LEDs, fuel cells
- **Scandium, Neodymium, Indium** — mentioned in US White House summit readout as target areas

## May 20, 2026: China Defends Controls as "Lawful"

China's Ministry of Commerce stated on May 20 that rare earth export controls are implemented "in accordance with laws and regulations" and that it will review applications that are "compliant and for civilian use." This was the first formal public defence of the controls as permanent legal policy — not a temporary bargaining chip.

The statement offered vague cooperation on "reasonable" US concerns but set no timeline, no specific controls to lift, and no verification mechanism. This confirms that rare earth controls are **structural policy**, not a negotiating position that can be traded away in tariff talks.

## Summit Outcome: No Deal

At the May 2026 Beijing summit, the White House claimed China agreed to "address" US shortages. China made no public confirmation. Trump left Beijing without a confirmed rare earth agreement. The export licensing slowdown continues.

This makes rare earths China's **most effective retained leverage** — it can ease or tighten controls unilaterally, independent of tariff discussions.

## The November 10, 2026 Risk

The October 2025 export-control package is suspended as part of the trade truce until November 10, 2026. If the truce lapses:

- Full export controls reactivate automatically
- Overseas defence users would not be granted licences
- Advanced-chip applications would be reviewed case-by-case
- Global manufacturing disruption would follow

This is a **hard date** for gold markets: a truce failure would trigger acute risk premium in gold, likely pushing prices sharply higher. See [[gold-geopolitical-risk-premium]].

## Why the US Cannot Quickly Diversify

The US has no near-term alternative to Chinese rare earths:
- Building domestic refining capacity takes 10–15 years
- Allied supply (Australia, Canada) covers only a fraction of demand
- The technology and processing expertise is concentrated in China after decades of investment

This asymmetry means China can use rare earths as a slow-burn economic weapon without risking its own strategic position — a structural source of uncertainty that benefits gold long-term.
