# Turkey Gold Reserves

**Summary**: Turkey sold ~127 tonnes of gold over H1 2026 (including 58.4t in a single two-week stretch worth ~$8bn) via gold swaps to defend the lira after the Feb 28, 2026 Iran war began — a domestic monetary-policy tool, not a structural bearish signal. Reserves fell to 534.85t in Q1 2026 from 613.70t in Q4 2025, and confirmed Q2 2026 data (IMF/WGC) shows a further modest decline to 530.62t — the earlier "rebuilding" reports have not shown up in the official quarterly figure yet.

**Last updated**: 2026-08-19

**Update 2026-08-19**: Confirmed Q2 2026 official reserve figure: **530.62t**, down ~4.2t from the 534.85t Q1 2026 print — a small further net reduction, not the "rebuilding" that some April/May reports had suggested. Net Q2 change is far smaller than the Q1 drawdown, consistent with the lira-defense swap activity having largely stabilised. | April 2026 holdings | ~535 tonnes (stabilised) |
| Q2 2026 official reserves (confirmed) | **530.62 tonnes** (down from 534.85t Q1) |

Turkey was the **largest net seller** of gold among central banks in Q1 2026, appearing alongside Russia and Azerbaijan on the net-seller side. Q2 2026 shows the reduction pace has slowed sharply but has not reversed into net buying at the quarterly level.

## Why Turkey Sold — And Why It's Not Bearish

Turkey's gold "sales" were predominantly conducted via **gold swap agreements** — a financing mechanism where gold is temporarily exchanged for foreign currency, with an obligation to return it. This is different from an outright sale:

- **Gold swaps** show up as a reduction in reserve holdings but do not permanently remove gold from Turkey's control
- They are used to manage **domestic lira liquidity** and satisfy local gold demand without depleting hard currency reserves
- Turkey has historically been a cyclical buyer and seller, using its gold reserve as a monetary policy lever

The stabilisation of holdings at ~535t in April 2026 confirms this was a transient adjustment, not a policy reversal away from gold.

## Context Within [[Central-Bank-Gold-Demand]]

Turkey's activity illustrates an important distinction for gold price modelling:

- **Structural buying** (Poland, China, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan): driven by long-term policy, persistent, price-inelastic
- **Tactical/cyclical activity** (Turkey): driven by domestic monetary conditions, temporary, should be analysed separately

Including Turkey's Q1 reduction in net demand figures without this context would overstate bearish supply and understate the true structural bid.
