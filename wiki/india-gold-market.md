# India Gold Market

**Summary**: India is the world's second-largest gold consumer and a key structural demand driver. The 2026 story is dominated by a 15% import duty hike (May 13), PM Modi's public appeal to defer purchases, a sharp shift from physical to digital gold, and a structurally slower RBI buying pace offset by large-scale repatriation.

## 2026 Policy Shock: 15% Import Duty

### What Changed

On **May 13, 2026**, the Government of India raised the import duty on gold from **6% to 15%** in a midnight notification — the sharpest single duty increase ever recorded. The simultaneous duty was applied to silver. (source: CNBC, BusinessToday)

### Why

India imports nearly all its gold in USD. High gold demand requires rupee-to-dollar conversion in the forex market, weakening the INR and widening the current account/trade deficit. The duty hike was explicitly designed to arrest the rupee slide and compress gold import volumes.

PM **Narendra Modi** made a direct public appeal on **May 10, 2026**, urging all Indian citizens to voluntarily defer non-essential gold purchases for a minimum of one year — an unusual personal intervention by the Prime Minister.

### Demand Impact

| Metric | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Expected full-year 2026 demand decline | ~50–60 tonnes (~10% y/y) |
| Channels most affected | Jewellery, bar and coin physical demand |
| Channels least affected | Gold ETFs (financial instruments, not physical imports) |
| Smuggling risk | WGC cautions high duty could revive illegal imports |

Investment demand surged in Q1 2026, driven by gold's strong price performance. ETFs hit record AUM levels. Jewellery volumes fell due to price sensitivity but spending rose as existing buyers maintained their patterns.

### Post-Duty-Hike (May 2026)

Physical demand channels (jewellery, bar, coin) are expected to slow materially through H2 2026. The duty hike + PM appeal creates a dual policy headwind for physical gold consumption.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|

| April 2026 inflows | ₹30.4bn ($325mn) — 12th consecutive positive month |
| April as % of January peak | ~13% — sharp moderation |
| May 13–18 flows | Net outflows/redemptions (profit-taking post-duty-hike news) |
| Largest ETF | Nippon India ETF Gold BeES — AUM ₹58,323 crore |
| #2/#3 | HDFC Gold ETF ₹23,239cr; SBI Gold ETF ₹24,550cr |

Key structural point: **import duty cannot suppress ETF inflows** because gold ETFs are financial instruments that do not physically import gold each time a unit is purchased. ETFs are therefore insulated from the policy headwind and are structurally more resilient. This is accelerating a shift from physical to digital gold in India.

- **Total: 880.52 tonnes**

The RBI has completed its primary accumulation phase (72.6t in 2024) and is now managing and repatriating its existing position.

## INR/USD Dynamics

The INR/USD exchange rate is a critical multiplier for domestic gold prices:

- **Current rate**: 1 USD = ~95.81 INR (May 25, 2026)
- **Effect**: Weaker rupee raises the INR cost of importing gold; at 95.81 vs. historical ~70–75 INR/USD levels, gold is structurally more expensive in rupee terms even at the same USD spot price
- **The 15% duty adds further cost**: total effective landed cost premium over international price is substantial
- **Dollar weakness** (DXY ~99) is providing partial support to INR, limiting further rupee depreciation

The government's gold import duty hike is both a demand-suppression tool and a rupee-support tool — two policy objectives achieved with one instrument.
