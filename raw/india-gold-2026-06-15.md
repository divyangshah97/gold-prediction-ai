## Retail & Jewellery Demand

Weak consumer sentiment at elevated prices:
- **Q1 2026 jewellery demand fell 19% YoY** to 66.1 tonnes (gold prices 81% above year-ago levels)
- For the first time in India's gold market history, **investment demand (82t) overtook jewellery demand (66t)** in Q1 2026
- Investment demand made up **54.3% of total consumption** (vs. historical ~25%)
- Bar and coin demand (66t) nearly matched jewellery demand — unprecedented
- **PM Modi's public appeal** (mid-May 2026) to defer gold purchases given "prohibitive" prices continues to suppress retail demand

With XAU/USD at $4,323 and domestic gold above ₹1,49,000/10g, physical jewellery demand remains under pressure from price sensitivity.

## ETF & Investment Flows

India gold ETFs Q1 2026: **record 20t net inflows** (record quarter), driven overwhelmingly by January surge (80% of flows in January alone).

However, trend has reversed in Q2 2026:
- **May 2026**: first net ETF outflow in 13 months — **₹725 crore outflow** (profit-booking + price correction from Jan ATH)
- **Multiple AMC restrictions in force**: HDFC, Nippon, ICICI Prudential, Tata AMC all capping gold ETF inflows:
  - FoF (Fund of Funds) route: ₹10 lakh/PAN/month cap
  - Direct ETF: ₹25 crore/day cap per AMC
  - First time 4+ major AMCs simultaneously restrict purchases
- This suggests supply constraint at the ETF level, not demand destruction — the AMCs are limiting themselves due to difficulty sourcing physical gold at domestic prices

No fresh June 2026 ETF flow data available as of June 15.

## Other India Gold News

- **Structural investment shift**: India Q1 2026 marked the first-ever quarter where gold investment demand exceeded jewellery demand — a fundamental shift in the world's most jewellery-intensive gold market. This reflects: (1) price sensitivity reducing physical jewellery affordability, (2) growing financial-market sophistication (ETFs, digital gold), (3) SEBI-registered gold schemes expanding retail access.
- **US-Iran peace deal (June 15)**: Lower oil prices (WTI $81/bbl) are directly positive for India's oil import bill and current account — a structural positive for INR, which could mildly dampen domestic gold prices in INR terms over coming weeks.
- **Gold as 3rd-largest import**: India's gold import bill Q1 2026 = 186t × ~$4,300/oz = ~$26B equivalent (annualised ~$100B+). This remains a significant current account pressure point even as the duty is meant to compress demand.
