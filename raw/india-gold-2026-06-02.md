## Import & Trade

**15% import duty in force** (effective May 13, 2026; basic customs duty 10% + AIDC 5%).

**May 14 update (still active)**: Government capped gold imports under the Advance Authorisation (AA) scheme at 100 kg — a tightening of norms to prevent misuse of duty-free import facility by jewellery exporters. (source: BusinessToday May 14, 2026)

The WGC's "India gold market update: Import tightening" (May 2026) confirms:
- Import duty hike from 6% to 15% is the steepest single increase on record.
- AA scheme cap at 100 kg for jewellery exporters is an additional regulatory tightening.
- Jewellery and bar-and-coin demand expected to decline 50–60 tonnes (~10% y/y) in full-year 2026.
- India's duty-free gold import cap for jewellery exporters has been reduced from unlimited to 100 kg per authorisation.

No new import data for May 2026 published yet. Most recent: April 2026 import figures expected mid-June 2026.

## Retail & Jewellery Demand

Physical gold demand remained severely suppressed in the fortnight ended May 27, 2026 (~7.5t vs ~25t in the prior year period — approximately **-70% year-on-year**). No new June data published yet; demand expected to remain depressed through Q2 2026.

The structural shift from physical gold (subject to 15% duty) to digital gold instruments (ETFs, sovereign gold bonds, digital gold platforms) is accelerating. Large chain jewellers have inventory buffers that provide some resilience for bridal demand, but discretionary purchases have all but ceased.

**PM Modi's public appeal** (May 10, 2026) to avoid gold purchases for a year continues to weigh on consumer sentiment.

- **15% import duty**: In force since May 13, 2026. No change.
- **AA scheme cap at 100 kg**: In force since May 14, 2026. No change.
- **Advance Authorisation scheme**: The government is tightening duty-free import norms for jewellery exporters — effectively reducing the channel through which exporters could bring in gold without paying the 15% duty.
- **No new policy announced June 2**.

The WGC's "Import tightening" assessment (May 2026) summarises the dual-instrument approach: (1) duty hike to raise the cost of gold imports; (2) AA scheme cap to close a regulatory loophole for exporters. Together, these are expected to significantly curtail official gold imports in 2026.

## Other India Gold News

- **Iran escalation impact on India**: Iran's suspension of US negotiations (June 1, 2026) re-elevates Strait of Hormuz risk. India imports ~85% of its crude oil needs; sustained Hormuz disruption keeps energy import costs elevated → weakens INR → raises domestic gold prices. This geopolitical linkage is a medium-term upside risk for MCX gold prices.
- **India as mediator**: India is NOT currently listed as a formal mediator in the US-Iran talks (Pakistan and Qatar are primary mediators), but India has strong diplomatic interests in a Hormuz resolution given its energy dependency.
- **Gold smuggling risk**: High duty (15%) historically correlates with increased grey-market imports. The WGC has flagged this risk. No data on May/June 2026 smuggling volumes yet.
