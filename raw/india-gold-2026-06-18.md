## RBI / Reserves

**MAJOR STORY: RBI categorically denies reports of gold sale.** In early June 2026, media reports circulated that the RBI may have sold approximately **$12 billion worth of gold** to defend forex reserves and support the rupee during the West Asia (Iran) conflict. The RBI formally denied these reports, stating gold holdings remain **unchanged at 880.52 metric tonnes**. The Finance Ministry also denied the reports. Gold's share in India's FX reserves has RISEN to 16.85% (as of May 22, 2026) — up from 13.92% in September 2025, precisely because gold value has appreciated in USD terms. RBI last bought near-zero quantities in 2026; the 880.52t was the March 31, 2026 figure.

Breakdown: 312.32t (Issue Department) + 568.20t (Banking Department). Repatriation: ~680t now domestic; ~200t still at Bank of England and BIS.

No new June purchases or sales confirmed.

## Import & Trade

Import duty: **15% basic customs duty** (effective May 13, 2026) + 5% AIDC + 3% GST = ~23% total landed cost premium. This was raised from 6% in May 2026 — the single steepest increase on record (+9 percentage points).

WGC India Gold Market Update (May 2026): The import duty hike is expected to reduce India's 2026 total gold demand by approximately **50–60 tonnes (~10% year-on-year)**. Q1 2026 imports were 186t (+58% YoY) — this was front-loading ahead of anticipated duty increases. Q2 2026 imports are expected to decline materially on duty-adjusted price levels.

Seasonally, the May 13–June period is inauspicious for purchases (summer wedding season has ended), which limits the full immediate impact of the duty hike.

## Retail & Jewellery Demand

Jewellery demand moderating due to: (1) record high prices (MCX near all-time highs in INR terms), (2) 15% import duty hike, (3) seasonal weakness post-wedding season. WGC expects jewellery + bar-and-coin demand decline of ~50–60t in FY2026 vs. FY2025. However, Q1 2026 saw investment demand (82t) overtake jewellery demand (66t) for the **first time ever** — structural shift underway. Major retailers: no specific June 18 news; demand broadly subdued.

India gold ETFs have attracted **$3.48 billion in net inflows YTD 2026** — among the highest on record. June 2026 recovery: **+₹2,081 crore** (rebounding from May's ₹725 crore outflow — first outflow in 13 months).

**6 AMCs have imposed large-subscription caps** on gold ETFs/FoFs:
- ICICI Prudential: ≥₹25cr direct (effective June 5)
- HDFC AMC: ≥₹25cr (effective June 8); ₹10L/PAN/month for FoF

No new AMC cap announcements found for June 18, 2026 specifically.

## INR Impact

**USD/INR: ₹94.4770** on June 18, 2026 — down 0.36% (INR STRENGTHENING). INR has rallied to multi-week highs as sharp decline in crude oil (WTI $75.49/bbl, -1.70%) significantly improves India's import outlook. India imports ~85% of its oil; lower crude = lower import bill = smaller current account deficit = stronger rupee.

INR at ₹94.48 is the strongest level since early 2026. A stronger INR is a natural hedge against rising gold prices for domestic buyers — it partially offsets global gold price increases in rupee terms. This explains why MCX settled at ₹1,52,748 (slightly below some estimates) despite XAU/USD rising.
