# US–China Beijing Summit — May 14–15, 2026

**Compiled**: 2026-05-21  
**Event**: Trump–Xi Jinping bilateral summit, Beijing  
**Significance**: First sitting US president to visit Beijing in nearly a decade

---

## Summit Overview

President Donald Trump and President Xi Jinping held two days of meetings in Beijing on May 14–15, 2026. The summit was characterised by observers as producing "meagre" results — a stabilisation of relations rather than a strategic breakthrough.

Xi called the meetings "historic" and a "landmark." Trump called them "fantastic trade deals." Independent analysts described the outcome as "underwhelming," noting limited concrete deliverables.

---

## What Was Agreed

### Trade & Commercial
- **Boeing aircraft**: China agreed to buy 200 Boeing jets — the first order of this kind from an official Chinese government arm in nearly a decade. Significantly below Trump's pre-summit expectation of 500 planes. Boeing shares fell **4%** on disappointment.
- **Agricultural goods**: Vague commitments to purchase US soybeans and other farm goods. White House cited $17 billion/year through 2028, but China provided no public confirmation of amounts.
- **Trade truce extended**: The tariff suspension from the November 2025 truce is extended. US tariff suspensions run until **November 10, 2026**. China's broader October 2025 export-control package suspended for the same period.
- **Rare earths (partial)**: White House claimed China agreed to "address" US shortages of yttrium, scandium, neodymium, and indium, and would address restrictions on rare-earth production equipment. No formal deal signed; China did not confirm specific commitments.
- **Bilateral mechanisms**: Agreement to establish a Board of Trade and Board of Investment to facilitate ongoing discussions.
- **Strategic framework**: Agreement to work toward a "constructive China-US relationship of strategic stability" as a guiding framework for the next three years.

### Semiconductors
- US reportedly licensed ~750,000 NVIDIA H200 chips to approximately 10 Chinese tech companies. If approved and delivered, this would roughly triple China's AI computing capacity. Xi told Trump "he wants to make his own chips" — indicating China sees this as a transitional arrangement.

---

## What Was NOT Agreed / Unresolved

| Issue | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Tariffs | Trump said tariffs "never came up meaningfully." No structural change. |
| Rare earths formal deal | Trump left Beijing without confirmed breakthrough. Exports still ~50% below pre-control levels. |
| Taiwan | No concessions. Xi warned of possible "clashes and even conflicts" if Taiwan not "handled properly." US maintained strategic ambiguity. |
| Iran | Trump claimed Xi offered help with Strait of Hormuz. China's foreign ministry did not confirm. No formal Iran cooperation agreement. |
| Political prisoners | No discussion reported. |
| Technology decoupling | No framework for reversing chip or tech restrictions. |

---

## Rare Earths: The Critical Background Issue

China controls:
- ~**90%** of global rare earth refining capacity
- **60%+** of global mined supply

Specific minerals with significantly reduced exports (currently ~50% below pre-control levels):
- Yttrium
- Dysprosium
- Terbium
- Scandium, Neodymium, Indium (mentioned in White House readout)

US assessment: "cannot out-mine, out-process or outspend China quickly enough to rebuild resilience in the near term." US is **years away** from supply chain resilience.

**November 10, 2026 deadline**: If the trade truce lapses, China's October 2025 export control package could reactivate — affecting defense, EV, and semiconductor supply chains globally.

| Pre-summit (3-week high) | $4,774 |
| During summit (May 14) | ~$4,700–$4,705 |
| Post-summit (May 15) | ~$4,553–$4,652 |
| Technical levels | 21d SMA $4,683 / 50d SMA $4,740 / 200d SMA $4,341 |

**Interpretation**: The summit reduced acute geopolitical risk premium. Gold declined as the worst-case US-China escalation scenario was taken off the table near-term. However, the structural safe-haven thesis remains intact — no major issue was resolved.

**Key supporting factor**: People's Bank of China has been buying gold for **17 consecutive months** as of May 2026, regardless of diplomatic developments. Summit changes nothing about PBoC's accumulation posture.
