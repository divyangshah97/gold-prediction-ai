## MCX Gold Price
MCX gold futures (August) opened at Rs 1,45,000 per 10 grams on June 25, 2026, down Rs 1,528 (-1.04%) from the previous close of Rs 1,46,529. By midday (~12:10 pm IST) the contract was trading at Rs 1,45,216, lower by Rs 1,313 (-0.9%). The decline mirrors the drop in COMEX gold to ~$3,975/oz as a stronger US dollar and hawkish Federal Reserve expectations weigh on global gold prices. Gold has now shed roughly 12% from its 2026 peak above Rs 1,65,000.

## RBI / Reserves
No new RBI gold data announced June 25. RBI gold holdings remain at 880.52t as confirmed after the June 2026 denial of a $12B sale story. The RBI repatriated 104t during FY26 (April 2025–March 2026); as of March 31, 2026, approximately 680t (~77%) of the 880t total is now stored domestically (Mumbai and Nagpur). The remaining ~200t is held in the UK (Bank of England). No change in headline tonnage.

## Import & Trade
India raised gold import duty from 6% to 15% (10% basic customs duty + 5% tax) effective May 13, 2026, to curb non-essential imports and reduce pressure on the rupee. Context: Gold imports had surged +81.7% YoY before the hike; silver imports had surged +157.2% YoY. Post-duty, gold demand fell sharply — IBJA reported fortnightly demand of ~7.5t (vs ~25t in same period last year, -70%). May 2026 gold import value fell 39% to $3.4B. The import duty hike is a structural negative for near-term physical demand.

## Retail & Jewellery Demand
Jewellery demand has been severely dampened by the combination of high domestic prices (import duty + weak rupee) and COMEX price volatility. IBJA data showed a ~70% drop in fortnightly demand post-duty hike. Retailers are holding minimal inventory. Wedding season demand is subdued relative to prior years. No new retail demand data for June 25 specifically.

## ETF & Investment Flows
Six AMCs have now imposed subscription caps on gold ETFs for large lump-sum investments (>Rs 25 crore direct / >Rs 10 lakh lump-sum for retail): HDFC, ICICI Prudential, Axis, Nippon India, Tata, and Aditya Birla Sun Life (6th to cap). These caps effective from early June 2026 target institutional/HNI investors; retail SIP investors unaffected. Despite May record outflows of Rs 7.25B, June 1–11 saw a recovery with inflows of Rs 16.31B (~Rs 1,631 crore), suggesting some institutional interest returned at lower prices. The ETF cap restrictions reflect supply-side constraints at AMCs, not a lack of retail demand.

## INR Impact
USD/INR: 94.47 on June 25, vs 94.6925 on June 24 — rupee slightly stronger by 0.23%. However, the rupee remains historically weak (down >7% YTD from ~88 at start of 2026), which structurally keeps MCX gold prices elevated relative to COMEX. On June 22, a rupee slip to 94.7 caused MCX gold to spike Rs 2,000. Today's marginally stronger rupee provides minor domestic price relief but the COMEX drop (-3.1%) dominates.

## Policy & Regulation
India gold import duty hike to 15% (effective May 13, 2026): 10% basic customs duty + 5% tax. This is the most significant policy change. Context: The 2024 budget had sharply reduced duty to 6% to support jewellery exports; the 2026 hike reverses that. No new government gold policy announced June 25.
