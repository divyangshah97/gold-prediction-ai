## ETF & Investment Flows

**May 2026**: India gold ETFs recorded their **first net outflow in 13 months** — ₹725 crore outflow (profit-booking after price correction from January peak). (source: Upstox)

**June 2026** (partial): Recovery underway — approximately ₹2,081 crore in ETF inflows as of mid-June (data captured June 16 update). This reversal suggests investors are treating the $4,300 level as an entry point after the correction from $5,589 ATH.

**ETF subscription restrictions remain active**: 6 AMCs (Nippon India, HDFC, ICICI Prudential, Tata, UTI, Aditya Birla Sun Life) are capping large inflows — Nippon: ₹10L/PAN/month (FoF); HDFC: ₹10L/PAN/month; ICICI & Tata: ₹25cr direct cap; Aditya Birla Sun Life effective June 9. These restrictions reflect government import-management intent.

No new AMC restriction announcements today.

## INR Impact

USD/INR fell to approximately **₹94.32** on June 17, 2026 (versus ~₹95.40 est. June 16), representing INR strengthening of ~1.1%. This is the **strongest INR level in recent sessions**, driven primarily by:
- WTI crude plunging 4.8% to $76.54/bbl on Iran-Hormuz reopening expectations (lower oil = smaller India import bill = INR-supportive)
- FOMC held rates at 3.50–3.75% (as expected, no hawkish shock) → USD steady but not strengthening

Impact on MCX: Despite INR strengthening (which typically compresses MCX prices in rupee terms), MCX gold still rose to ~₹1,53,000 because the XAU/USD gain (+$18.67) outweighed the INR appreciation effect.

Year-to-date: INR has depreciated ~7% in 2026 (from ~₹88 at 2025 year-end), structurally raising the rupee cost of gold imports. Today's INR recovery to 94.32 is partial.
