## Import & Trade

**PM Modi's gold-buying restraint appeal reaffirmed**: first made May 10, 2026 (one of seven steps to protect the economy from the global oil shock, delivered in Hyderabad), renewed in early September 2026. The government's stated aim is to conserve foreign exchange, support the rupee, and encourage circulation of India's estimated ~31,000 tonnes of household-held gold. The **15% import duty** (raised from 6% — a 10% basic customs duty plus a 5% agriculture infrastructure and development cess, the steepest single increase on record, effective since May 13, 2026) remains in place. **New this window**: Gems & Jewellery Council chairman Rajesh Rokde told ET Online that discussions are ongoing about a possible cut to the duty, after the higher rate failed to curb imports and instead contributed to growth in the grey market. The government had estimated the hike could reduce official imports 15-20% and save $10-15bn in forex outflows, though analysts note import volumes would need to fall ~47% to bring the import bill back to FY25 levels at current ~$4,700/oz gold prices.

## Retail & Jewellery Demand

Q2 2026 data (World Gold Council, carried forward as the latest available): India's gold demand softened to **131t** (-6% y/y, -13% q/q) even as spend hit a quarterly record of **₹1,979 billion (~$21bn)** on higher prices. Investment demand (bars/coins) surged **54% y/y to 82t**, now accounting for ~70% of total net demand — a structural shift away from jewellery toward investment gold. Retailers report 40-60% of jewellery transactions are now exchange-for-old-gold deals, as consumers trade in existing holdings to offset higher prices; lightweight jewellery with lower making charges is gaining share as ticket sizes shrink.
