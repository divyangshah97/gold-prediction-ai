## India — Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

### Current Holdings (March 2026)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total gold reserves | 880.52 tonnes |
| Domestic holdings (in India) | 680.05 tonnes |
| Overseas holdings (BoE, BIS) | 197.67 tonnes |

| Net Q1 2026 purchase | ~0.34 tonnes (essentially flat) |

### Historical Progression

| Period | Total | Domestic | Overseas |
|---|---|---|---|
| March 2023 | 794.64 t | 301.10 t | 437.22 t |
| March 2024 | 822.10 t | 408.31 t | — |
| September 2025 | 880.8 t | 575.8 t | 290.3 t |
| March 2025 | 879.59 t | 511.99 t | 348.62 t |
| March 2026 | 880.52 t | 680.05 t | 197.67 t |

### Key Trend: Repatriation, Not New Purchases

- **2024**: RBI purchased 72.6 tonnes — peak acquisition year
- **2025**: RBI purchased only 4 tonnes — lowest in 8 years
- **2026 (Q1)**: Net purchase near zero (0.34 tonnes)
- **Major activity in 2026 is repatriation**: ~168 metric tonnes brought back from overseas vaults (BoE, BIS) in the 12 months to March 2026
- Overseas holdings have collapsed from 437t (March 2023) → 197t (March 2026) as gold is repatriated domestically

### Gold as % of FX Reserves
- September 2025: ~13.9% of total FX reserves
- September 2024: ~9%
- The increase is driven by gold price appreciation, not new buying volume
- Total FX reserves value as of March 2026: over $691 billion; gold portion: ~$108 billion

### Strategic Context
The RBI front-loaded its gold buying in 2024 (72.6t). The 2026 focus has shifted to:
1. Repatriating gold from London (geopolitical insurance post-Russia sanctions)
2. Reducing overseas custodial risk (Bank of England exposure)
3. No urgency to buy new gold at current elevated prices

## Russia — Bank of Russia / National Wealth Fund

### Current Holdings

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Reserves as of April 1, 2026 | 74.1 million troy oz (~2,304 t) |
| Reserves as of January 1, 2026 | 74.8 million troy oz (~2,326 t) |
| Net change YTD (Jan–Apr 2026) | −700,000 oz (~−21.8 t) |
| Reserves value (early April 2026) | $334 billion |
| Reserves value (January 1, 2026) | $325 billion |

### Selling Activity in 2026

- Russia has sold **~21.8 tonnes (700,000 oz)** of gold YTD as of April 2026
- Selling began in **November 2025** via National Wealth Fund (NWF) operations
- Official stated reason: gold price appreciation had increased gold's proportion of total reserves beyond target — "diversification" rationale
- Real driver: war-related fiscal pressures; Russia's **federal budget deficit reached $61.3 billion**

### National Wealth Fund (NWF) Context

- Before the 2022 invasion, NWF held **405.7 tonnes** of gold
- Since then, **232.6 tonnes** have been sold to cover budget spending
- Current NWF gold position not separately disclosed
- Q1 2026 data in prior raw file also noted 22t reduction for Russia

### Why Russia Is Selling Despite Being a Major Holder

1. **Sanctions isolation**: Dollar and euro assets are frozen/inaccessible. Gold and yuan are now Russia's most liquid reserve assets.
2. **Fiscal deficit**: War spending requires converting reserves to domestic currency. Gold is one of the few fungible assets available.
3. **Price opportunity**: With gold at multi-year highs, Russia is monetising reserves at elevated prices.
4. **Rebalancing**: Gold's price appreciation means it now represents a higher % of total reserves than intended.

### Geopolitical Note
Russia selling does not reflect a negative view on gold — it reflects forced liquidation under sanctions. It is qualitatively different from a market participant selling on bearish price expectations.
